Considerations

  • Killed or recombinant Rabies vaccinationis strongly recommended for all shelter and community dogs and cats in compliance with relevant state and local regulations, at the first encounter. For shelter animals, when administration at intake is not feasible, vaccination of eligible animals should occur as soon as possible but especially before leaving the facility (e.g. to adoption, foster or community events, or transfer to another shelter). For underage animals unlikely to receive a vaccine at a later date (e.g. part of TNR/SNR/RTF program or single-event clinic in an underserved area) the rabies vaccine is safe to administer and will likely provide protection, though it will not be recognized as valid when determining vaccine status and no rabies certificate should be provided.

What the labels mean

Which vaccines

Core
Considered essential for all animals in that context category, and should be given at intake or as soon as possible. (Guideline definition)
Situational
May have good evidence for use under particular circumstances, but not appropriate for all shelter or community animals. (Guideline definition)

How certain the evidence is

Evidence rating
The task force's own rating of the evidence behind each recommendation, from 0 to 4, recorded when the recommendations were voted. Higher means more certain. A range means the statement rests on more than one voted recommendation and they were rated differently.
Not rated
No rating was recorded for this statement, usually because it was proposed after the vote.

Kinds of evidence

  • ■ Experimental
  • ● Observational
  • ▲ Case report or series
  • ◆ Expert or consensus
  • ■ filled: independent or not stated   □ hollow: manufacturer funded, authored, or supplied
  • (species) study in a different species
  • (model) decision model
  • Italic no study cited: Inference (reasoned from cited claims), Consensus (the task force's judgment), Not assembled (an evidence gap)

Limits of the evidence for this claim

Limits of the evidence for this claim
Failure was 4.12% (Mansfield 2004) and 5.89% in dogs vaccinated in Italy (Rota Nodari 2017). Over 90% of dogs that failed reached adequate titers after a booster (Wallace 2017). Antibody outcome, and titers do not directly correlate with protection

Limits of the evidence for this claim

ClaimCitation
Adverse events after rabies vaccination are uncommon and mostly mild ● (dog)
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Dogs only, events within 3 days only. Rabies vaccine given alone had 24.7 events per 10,000 doses, and risk rose in dogs under 10 kg and with more vaccines per visit
Rabies vaccination of animals under 12 weeks is safe ◇, ◆
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Consensus statements. No cited study reports adverse events in vaccinated animals under 12 weeks

Field effectiveness

ClaimCitation
Rabies in vaccinated dogs and cats is rare, and rarer still in currently vaccinated animals ●, ●, ▲ (multiple domestic species), ▲ (multiple domestic species), ▲ (multiple domestic species)
Limits of the evidence for this claim
From 2002 to 2022, 39 of 1,525 rabid dogs and 30 of 5,530 rabid cats had any vaccination history, with 15 true failures (Nathan 2025). Surveillance depends on reporting and vaccination records

ClaimCitation
