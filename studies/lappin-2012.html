ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Lappin 2012

Full reference
Lappin MR. 2012. Feline panleukopenia virus, feline herpesvirus-1 and feline calicivirus antibody responses in seronegative specific pathogen-free kittens after parenteral administration of an inactivated FVRCP vaccine or a modified live FVRCP vaccine. Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery 14(2):161-164.
DOI
10.1177/1098612X11432240
Design
controlled serology study (two vaccine arms, no challenge)
Species
cat
Animals
12 SPF kittens (6 inactivated Fel-O-Vax PCT, 6 MLV Fel-O-Guard Plus 3)
Population
seronegative SPF kittens vaccinated SQ on days 0, 14 and 28
Funding
manufacturer. Fort Dodge Animal Health (funding statement). Both vaccines tested are Fort Dodge / Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica products. Author declares no conflict
Study's main limitation
Six kittens per arm, antibody outcome only, and the arms differed only at day 7.
What it reports
Kittens given MLV were more likely to have seroconverted to FPV on day 7 than those given inactivated vaccine, and all kittens in both groups had seroconverted by day 14.

Claims citing this study

  • MLV produces protective titers sooner than inactivated vaccine MLV FVRCP
  • Nearly all unprotected adult cats seroconvert after one MLV dose MLV FVRCP
  • MLV vaccine raised FCV titers sooner than inactivated vaccine in kittens with low maternal antibody. MLV FVRCP
  • MLV vaccine raised FCV titers sooner than inactivated vaccine in kittens with low maternal antibody. Intranasal herpesvirus and calicivirus (MLV)
  • MLV vaccine raised FCV titers sooner than inactivated vaccine in kittens with low maternal antibody. Dual-strain killed calicivirus
