ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Schatzberg 2003

Full reference
Schatzberg SJ, Haley NJ, Barr SC, Parrish C, Steingold S, Summers BA, deLahunta A, Kornegay JN, Sharp NJH. 2003. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification of parvoviral DNA from the brains of dogs and cats with cerebellar hypoplasia. Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine 17(4):538-544.
DOI
10.1111/j.1939-1676.2003.tb02475.x
Design
case series with PCR and sequencing of archival tissue
Species
dog and cat
Animals
8 cats and 2 dog littermates with cerebellar hypoplasia, plus 24 canine and feline comparison or control cases
Population
archival paraffin-embedded brains
Funding
independent. Cornell in-house research grant, with preliminary support from NC State College of Veterinary Medicine (acknowledgments)
Study's main limitation
Two affected puppies from one litter, in a different species, with retrospective records.
What it reports
Parvoviral DNA was amplified from all 8 cats and both puppies with cerebellar hypoplasia. The puppies' dam was vaccinated for parvovirus early in pregnancy, but the sequence was a wild-type CPV-2b, not the vaccine strain of the time.
