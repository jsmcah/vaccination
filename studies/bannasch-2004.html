ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Bannasch 2004

Full reference
Bannasch MJ, Foley JE. 2005. Epidemiologic evaluation of multiple respiratory pathogens in cats in animal shelters. Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery 7(2):109-119.
DOI
10.1016/j.jfms.2004.07.004
Design
cross-sectional serosurvey (nearest label. Actually a cross-sectional pathogen survey with unmatched case-control analysis of URI, using virus isolation and PCR rather than serology)
Species
cat
Animals
573 cats (314 with URI, 259 without)
Population
cats in eight California shelters (four traditional, three no kill, one large traditional) sampled in 2001 and 2002, including isolation, general population, feral and foster cats
Funding
independent. Maddie's Shelter Medicine Program UC Davis and the Center for Companion Animal Health (acknowledgments)
Study's main limitation
FCV detected by virus isolation only, so vaccine-like strains and carriers cannot be separated from causal infection, and some shelters contributed very few cats.
What it reports
Overall FCV isolation was 28.1% (95% CI 24.5 to 32.0) of all cats sampled. The abstract gives FCV prevalence of 13 to 36% across shelters, while the results text gives 0 to 67% (the 67% was from 6 cats). FCV was not associated with URI on univariate analysis (OR 1.04, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.51, P = 0.85). Some of 27 sequenced isolates clustered with the F9 vaccine strain.
