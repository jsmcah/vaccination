ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Poulet 2000

Full reference
Poulet H, Brunet S, Soulier M, Leroy V, Goutebroze S, Chappuis G. 2000. Comparison between acute oral/respiratory and chronic stomatitis/gingivitis isolates of feline calicivirus: pathogenicity, antigenic profile and cross-neutralisation studies. Archives of Virology 145(2):243-261.
DOI
10.1007/s007050050021
Design
experimental study (oronasal infection of unvaccinated SPF kittens with field isolates to raise antisera and describe pathogenicity, plus monoclonal antibody profiling and two-way cross-neutralization of 23 isolates, no vaccination and no challenge of vaccinated cats)
Species
cat
Animals
2 SPF kittens per isolate (23 isolates), with clinical monitoring for the 14 French and British isolates (28 kittens)
Population
SPF kittens 10 to 14 weeks old infected with 8 French, 6 British and 9 US field isolates from cats with acute oral or respiratory disease or chronic gingivitis or stomatitis
Funding
manufacturer. All authors at Merial, Lyon (author affiliation). No funding statement
Study's main limitation
Two kittens per isolate, some observed only on selected days, and no vaccinated animals, so it says nothing directly about vaccine cross-protection.
What it reports
All tested isolates caused acute disease after oronasal inoculation, with fever, apathy, oral ulcers, gingivitis, rhinitis or conjunctivitis. Two kittens died or were euthanized (strains 388b and J5) with severe pneumonia at necropsy. The highest clinical scores were with strains 431, H1-4, J5, F3, 388b and F3031, and ulcers were most severe with 431 and F1. There was no difference in total clinical score between chronic and acute isolates (mean 5.9 vs 7.1, p = 0.19). Chronic isolates had lower mean bilateral antigenic relatedness than acute isolates (p = 0.001), and cross-neutralization was greater within country than between countries.
