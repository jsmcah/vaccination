ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Pedersen 1995

Full reference
Pedersen NC, Hawkins KF. 1995. Mechanisms for persistence of acute and chronic feline calicivirus infections in the face of vaccination. Veterinary Microbiology 47(1-2):141-156.
DOI
10.1016/0378-1135(95)00101-F
Design
experimental infection study (oral inoculation of SPF cats with vaccine and field strains, with in vitro cross-neutralization)
Species
cat
Animals
small groups (4 to 6 cats per vaccine strain, 19 FCV-Cook and 4 FCV-VacNor immunized cats with age-matched controls)
Population
SPF cats 12 to 16 weeks old and older colony cats, UC Davis
Funding
independent. Robert H. Winn Foundation, with support from Ralston-Purina (acknowledgements)
Study's main limitation
Small groups, oral inoculation rather than natural exposure, and oral culturing stopped early for one strain.
What it reports
Oral inoculation with live vaccine strains caused acute disease and oral shedding, with two cats still shedding at 12 weeks. After field-strain challenge, shedding lasted 5 to 6 weeks in 8 of 9 cats and one cat shed at 10 weeks. Prior infection lessened disease but did not prevent the chronic carrier state.
