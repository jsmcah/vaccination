ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Kalli 2010

Full reference
Kalli I, Leontides LS, Mylonakis ME, Adamama-Moraitou K, Rallis T, Koutinas AF. 2010. Factors affecting the occurrence, duration of hospitalization and final outcome in canine parvovirus infection. Research in Veterinary Science 89:174-178.
DOI
10.1016/j.rvsc.2010.02.013
Design
retrospective observational matched case-control study
Species
dog
Animals
94 cases and 188 age-matched controls
Population
puppies with natural CPV enteritis and age-matched hospital controls (healthy or with unrelated conditions) at a veterinary teaching hospital in Thessaloniki, Greece
Funding
not stated. No funding or acknowledgement statement found in the PDF
Study's main limitation
Hospital-based case-control study with owner-reported vaccination history (unknown for 6 cases), so vaccination is a crude ever versus never measure.
What it reports
Puppies vaccinated against CPV at least once had 2.13 times lower odds of CPV enteritis than unvaccinated puppies (95% CI 1.1 to 4.2, P < 0.001). 45 of 94 cases (48%) had been vaccinated at least once. 59 of 80 cases with known outcome (73.7%) survived.
