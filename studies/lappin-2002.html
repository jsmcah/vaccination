ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Lappin 2002

Full reference
Lappin MR, Andrews J, Simpson D, Jensen WA. 2002. Use of serologic tests to predict resistance to feline herpesvirus 1, feline calicivirus, and feline parvovirus infection in cats. Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association 220(1):38-42.
DOI
10.2460/javma.2002.220.38
Design
experimental challenge study (serology vs outcome of virulent challenge in vaccinated and unvaccinated laboratory cats, plus a cross-sectional serosurvey of client-owned cats)
Species
cat
Animals
72 laboratory-reared cats in four challenge experiments and 276 client-owned cats (serosurvey only). FCV challenge used experiments 1, 3 and 4 (38 vaccinated, 18 unvaccinated).
Population
Laboratory-reared cats housed in an SPF facility for challenge. Client-owned cats with unknown vaccination history for the serosurvey.
Funding
manufacturer. Supported by Heska Corp (title page footnote). Three of four authors are Heska employees, and Heska made the IN vaccine and the ELISA studied (affiliations, footnotes)
Study's main limitation
Every vaccinated cat was FCV seropositive and resistant, so negative predictive value could not be estimated. Small control groups and manufacturer funding add to this.
What it reports
In FCV challenge 9 to 36 months after vaccination, all 38 vaccinated cats (14 given one IN MLV dose, 24 given SQ MLV or killed vaccine 2 to 4 times) were FCV VN and ELISA positive and resistant (relative efficacy above 50%). Mean relative efficacy was 92% after IN and 94% after SQ vaccination. Positive predictive value was 100% for both FCV assays. Two of 18 unvaccinated, ELISA-negative controls were also resistant.

Claims citing this study

  • Cats given one IN MLV dose resisted FCV challenge 11 months later. MLV FVRCP
  • Cats given one IN MLV dose resisted FCV challenge 11 months later. Intranasal herpesvirus and calicivirus (MLV)
  • Cats given one IN MLV dose resisted FCV challenge 11 months later. Dual-strain killed calicivirus
