ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Wensman 2015

Full reference
Wensman JJ, Samman A, Lindhe A, Thibault JC, Berndtsson LT, Hosie MJ. 2015. Ability of vaccine strain induced antibodies to neutralize field isolates of caliciviruses from Swedish cats. Acta Veterinaria Scandinavica 57:86.
DOI
10.1186/s13028-015-0178-z
Design
experimental study (in vitro cross-neutralization of field isolates by antisera raised by infecting cats with vaccine strains, no vaccination and no challenge, nearest category)
Species
cat
Animals
78 FCV field isolates tested against 8 antisera from 8 cats (one pair infected with each of F9, 255, G1 and 431)
Population
isolates from Swedish cats with clinical signs compatible with FCV, submitted to the National Veterinary Institute October 2008 to February 2012
Funding
manufacturer. Serological testing funded by Merial, and one author (Thibault) a Merial employee (acknowledgements and competing interests). Samples collected independently by the National Veterinary Institute
Study's main limitation
Antisera came from oronasal infection of one pair of cats per strain, not from vaccinated cats, and the two cats in each pair often disagreed widely. In vitro only, with no link to protection.
What it reports
At titer 5 or more, paired antisera neutralized 20.5% and 11.5% of isolates (F9), 47.4% and 64.1% (255), 33.3% and 70.5% (G1), and 69.2% and 89.7% (431). Both F9 antisera neutralized 10% of isolates, both 255 antisera 44%, and the G1 plus 431 combination 73%. A hypothetical 431 plus 255 combination neutralized 77% (60/78).

  • Antisera to the F9 strain neutralized about a quarter of field isolates, against about four fifths for dual-strain antigens. Intranasal herpesvirus and calicivirus (MLV)
