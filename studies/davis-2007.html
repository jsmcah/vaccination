ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Davis 2007

Full reference
Davis R, Jayappa H, Abdelmagid OY, Armstrong R, Sweeney D, Lehr C. 2007. Comparison of the mucosal immune response in dogs vaccinated with either an intranasal avirulent live culture or a subcutaneous antigen extract vaccine of Bordetella bronchiseptica. Veterinary Therapeutics 8(1):32-40.
Design
experimental challenge study (randomized, placebo controlled, observers masked)
Species
dog
Animals
35 puppies in three groups (IN trivalent, SC antigen extract given twice 21 days apart, saline placebo IN and SC). Group sizes are not stated, but reported percentages imply about 12, 11 and 12
Population
healthy beagle puppies 9 to 10 weeks old with no Bb vaccination history and low Bb agglutinating antibody
Funding
manufacturer. Funded by Schering-Plough Animal Health, and all authors are Schering-Plough employees. The IN product tested (Intra-Trac 3) is the sponsor's, and the SC comparator (Bronchicine CAe) is a Pfizer product (funding footnote and affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Single challenge 63 days after vaccination in a sponsor-run study comparing the sponsor's IN product against a competitor's SC product, with small groups and group sizes not reported.
What it reports
IN vaccinated dogs developed Bb-specific nasal IgA (significant versus other groups on days 42 and 56, P <= 0.0002), while SC antigen extract and placebo dogs had no measurable nasal IgA. After challenge on day 63, IN dogs had lower mean cough scores than SC (P = 0.0009) or placebo dogs (P = 0.0058), and fewer days coughing (1.3 versus 5.9 and 4.8 days). SC dogs did not differ from placebo (P = 0.51). Only 4 IN dogs (33%) shed low numbers of challenge organisms, versus all SC dogs and 11 of 12 placebo dogs.
