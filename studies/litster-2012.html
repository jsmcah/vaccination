ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Litster 2012

Full reference
Litster A, Nichols J, Volpe A. 2012. Prevalence of positive antibody test results for canine parvovirus (CPV) and canine distemper virus (CDV) and response to modified live vaccination against CPV and CDV in dogs entering animal shelters. Veterinary Microbiology 157(1-2):86-90.
DOI
10.1016/j.vetmic.2011.12.030
Design
prospective field serology study
Species
dog
Animals
102 dogs (51 per shelter)
Population
dogs 4 months and older entering two Chicago-area shelters
Funding
independent. Maddie's Fund grant, with test kits from Synbiotics (acknowledgments)
Study's main limitation
Point-of-care ELISA, only negatives were retested, and Shelter 1 dogs had been vaccinated 5 days before enrolment.
What it reports
CDV antibody was positive in 37.3% and 41.2% at intake, 75.0% and 67.4% by day 6 to 8, and 93.8% and 97.8% by day 13 to 15 after MLV.

Claims citing this study

  • Most dogs entering shelters had CDV antibody within 2 weeks of one MLV dose MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
  • Roughly half of dogs entering shelters have no measurable CDV antibody MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
  • Many dogs arrive at shelters without protective CPV antibody MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
  • Nearly all shelter dogs have CPV antibody 1 to 2 weeks after one MLV dose at intake MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
  • About two thirds of dogs arrive already protected, so population immunity starts high MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
