ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Ellis 2002

Full reference
Ellis JA, Krakowka GS, Dayton AD, Konoby C. 2002. Comparative efficacy of an injectable vaccine and an intranasal vaccine in stimulating Bordetella bronchiseptica-reactive antibody responses in seropositive dogs. Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association 220(1):43-48.
Design
controlled serology study (randomized complete block, unvaccinated controls, no challenge)
Species
dog
Animals
40 dogs (10 per group, unvaccinated, SC, IN, SC plus IN)
Population
laboratory beagles about 1 year old with a variable history of Bb vaccination (last dose 287 to 320 days earlier), all seropositive at the start
Funding
manufacturer. Supported by Pfizer Animal Health, maker of both vaccines tested (CoughGuard B and NasaGuard-B), and one author (Dayton) is a Pfizer employee (title page footnote and author affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Antibody outcomes only with no challenge, in previously vaccinated laboratory beagles, and salivary IgA measurement was highly variable.
What it reports
A single SC dose (alone or with IN) raised serum Bb IgG above unvaccinated and IN-only dogs from days 5 to 6 through day 28. IN alone raised serum IgG above controls from day 10 but remained lower than SC groups. Neither vaccine changed salivary IgA or IgG. Serum IgA was higher in SC groups by day 6 and in all vaccinated groups by day 10, and no adverse effects were seen.
