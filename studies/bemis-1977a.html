ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Bemis 1977a

Full reference
Bemis DA, Greisen HA, Appel MJG. 1977. Pathogenesis of canine bordetellosis. Journal of Infectious Diseases 135(5):753-762.
Design
experimental challenge study (pathogenesis, aerosol infection, with a small vaccination and challenge experiment)
Species
dog
Animals
about 100 dogs in several experiments, including 64 exposed to strain 87, 7 to other strains, 5 to high-passage strain 87, 9 to mixed bacterial aerosols, 14 to non-infectious aerosols, and 6 given IM formalin-killed bacterin (1 sacrificed for serum, the rest challenged against unvaccinated controls). Exact total not stated
Population
specific pathogen-free beagles 6 to 12 weeks old from the Baker Institute colony, Cornell University
Funding
independent. Supported by the Whitehall Foundation and the John M. Olin Foundation (acknowledgement footnote). Authors at the James A. Baker Institute for Animal Health, Cornell University (author affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Vaccination experiment is a small add-on with group sizes stated inconsistently (5 vaccinates challenged in methods, 3 of 4 vaccinates reported in results), and the vaccine was a laboratory-made adjuvanted whole-cell bacterin, not a commercial product.
What it reports
Aerosol Bb alone caused tracheobronchitis in 56 of 64 SPF dogs, with cough starting 2 to 10 days after infection and Bb persisting in the trachea up to 14 weeks. Recovered dogs resisted reinfection at 8 to 16 weeks despite low or negative serum agglutinin titers, and 3 dogs were susceptible again 14 months after infection. Two IM doses of formalin-killed bacterin produced titers peaking at 1:250, but 3 of 4 vaccinated and 2 of 3 control dogs developed signs after challenge, with no difference in tracheal bacterial counts.
