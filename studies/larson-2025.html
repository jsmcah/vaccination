Larson L, Hansen J, Ramasami P, Miller JM, Slagter BJ, Tremblay D, Patel M, Wasmoen T. 2025. Prophylactic use of canine parvovirus monoclonal antibody induces blockade of vaccinal canine parvovirus immunization similar to maternally derived passive immunity. American Journal of Veterinary Research (published online September 26, 2025).

Design
experimental study (randomized, CPMA versus saline, serology only)
Species
dog
Animals
20 purpose-bred beagles (13 CPMA, 7 controls)
Population
CPV-seronegative pups 7 to 8 weeks old
Funding
manufacturer. Funded entirely by Elanco Animal Health, maker of CPMA, and six of eight authors are Elanco staff (funding statement, affiliations)
Study's main limitation
Small purpose-bred group with no maternal antibody, funded and largely authored by the CPMA maker.
What it reports
Prophylactic CPMA gave a mean passive HI titer of 1674 with a half-life of 18.9 days and blocked response to MLV CPV vaccine for 15 weeks (12 of 13 seroconverted after the fourth dose on day 105). All 7 control pups seroconverted within 7 days of their first MLV DAPP dose (HI 2560 to 10,240). Authors suggest extending the series to 17 weeks after CPMA.

Claims citing this study

  • One MLV dose produces protective antibody in seronegative dogs within about 4 days MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
  • Puppies given CPV monoclonal antibody cannot respond to the parvo component for about 15 weeks MLV DAPP, DAP, or DP
