ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Huang 2010

Full reference
Huang C, Hess J, Gill M, Hustead D. 2010. A dual-strain feline calicivirus vaccine stimulates broader cross-neutralization antibodies than a single-strain vaccine and lessens clinical signs in vaccinated cats when challenged with a homologous feline calicivirus strain associated with virulent systemic disease. Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery 12(2):129-137.
DOI
10.1016/j.jfms.2009.08.006
Design
experimental challenge study (double blind randomized vaccinate vs control, homologous VS-FCV challenge, plus in vitro cross-neutralization with pooled sera)
Species
cat
Animals
49 kittens in the challenge study (20 dual-strain vaccine, 20 single-strain active control, 9 unvaccinated challenge controls), plus pooled sera from 11 and 40 vaccinated SPF cats and 40 cats used to test field isolate virulence
Population
8-week-old American shorthair kittens seronegative to FCV, FPV, FHV-1, FCP and FeLV, gang housed
Funding
manufacturer. All authors at Fort Dodge Animal Health, maker of the vaccines tested (Fel-O-Vax Lv-K IV and CaliciVax) (author affiliation). No funding statement
Study's main limitation
Challenge was homologous to the VS-FCV strain in the vaccine, and the 20 single-strain active controls were not challenged, so the challenge does not compare dual-strain and single-strain protection. Cross-neutralization used pooled sera at a low cutoff (1:2).
What it reports
Killed vaccines were given SQ twice 3 weeks apart and kittens were challenged oronasally with FCV-DD1 2 weeks after the second dose. All 9 unvaccinated controls developed VSD (one found dead day 9), for a mean of 10.5 days (95% CI 8.9 to 12.32). None of 20 dual-strain vaccinates developed VSD and their signs lasted 1 day. Prevented fraction was 100% (95% CI 83.2% to 100%). Pooled dual-strain antisera neutralized 30/43 (70%) isolates versus 10/43 (23%) for single-strain antisera, and 6/9 (67%) versus 1/9 (11%) of VS-FCV isolates.
