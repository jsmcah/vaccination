ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Wallace 2017

Full reference
Wallace RM, Pees A, Blanton JB, Moore SM. 2017. Risk factors for inadequate antibody response to primary rabies vaccination in dogs under one year of age. PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases 11(7):e0005761.
DOI
10.1371/journal.pntd.0005761
Design
retrospective observational (laboratory serology records)
Species
dog
Animals
8,011 dogs
Population
dogs under 1 year with no prior vaccination whose serum was sent to a reference laboratory
Funding
independent. No specific funding (funding statement)
Study's main limitation
Laboratory submission population with variable sampling times.
What it reports
Dogs vaccinated after 16 weeks had higher GMT (1.64 IU/mL) than those vaccinated under 12 weeks (1.22) or at 12 to 16 weeks (1.21). Over 90% of dogs that failed reached adequate titers after a booster.

Claims citing this study

  • A minority of dogs fail to reach 0.5 IU/mL after a first dose, and most respond to a booster Rabies (killed or recombinant)
  • Young animals respond less well to a first rabies dose, which supports a booster within a year Rabies (killed or recombinant)
  • Dogs first vaccinated before 12 weeks had titers similar to dogs vaccinated at 12 to 16 weeks Rabies (killed or recombinant)
