ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Anderton 2004

Full reference
Anderton TL, Maskell DJ, Preston A. 2004. Ciliostasis is a key early event during colonization of canine tracheal tissue by Bordetella bronchiseptica. Microbiology 150:2843-2855.
DOI
10.1099/mic.0.27283-0
Design
experimental study (in vitro air-interface organ culture of canine tracheal tissue)
Species
dog (tracheal tissue)
Animals
tracheae from healthy beagles aged 9 months to 4 years used as naive controls in other studies. Number of dogs not stated, six independent experiments, more than 20 organ culture pieces per inoculum in the bead clearance assay
Population
ex vivo canine tracheal explants infected with wild-type Bb (RB50 and a kennel cough isolate SB796) or isogenic bvg mutants
Funding
independent. Wellcome Trust Programme Grant 054588 and a Wellcome Trust Prize Studentship (acknowledgements). Tracheae obtained with the cooperation of GlaxoSmithKline and University of Cambridge groups (methods)
Study's main limitation
In vitro tissue model without immune cells or inflammatory response, so it describes early pathogenesis but cannot address clinical disease, coinfection or vaccination.
What it reports
Wild-type Bb at 10^8 cfu stopped ciliary bead clearance in all organ cultures within 1 hour, before mucus production or visible epithelial damage. At 10^5 cfu clearance was lost in 18% (RB50) and 35% (SB796) of cultures, and at 10^2 cfu in none. bvg mutants did not colonize or cause ciliostasis but induced similar mucus release. No vaccination data.
