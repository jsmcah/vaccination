ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Sant'Anna da Costa 2022

Full reference
Sant'Anna da Costa R, Di Azevedo MIN, dos Santos Baptista Borges AL, Aymée L, Martins G, Lilenbaum W. 2022. Effect of vaccination against Leptospira on shelter asymptomatic dogs following a long-term study. Animals 12(14):1788.
DOI
10.3390/ani12141788
Design
prospective controlled field study (single dose, natural exposure, no challenge)
Species
dog
Animals
118 (94 vaccinated once with one of three vaccines, 24 unvaccinated controls)
Population
adult dogs in one shelter near Rio de Janeiro, an area endemic for leptospirosis, seronegative and PCR negative at entry, no lepto vaccine for at least 12 months
Funding
independent. No external funding (funding statement)
Study's main limitation
Very high natural exposure, so a single dose may have acted on primed dogs. Controls also seroconverted. Two of three vaccines were bivalent. No clinical cases and no culture
What it reports
PCR positive at least once in 44 of 94 vaccinated (46.8%) and 18 of 24 controls (75%) (OR 0.3, 95% CI 0.1 to 0.8). Repeat positives in 4 of 94 and 4 of 24. MAT reactive at least once in 69 of 94 vaccinated (73.4%) and 9 of 24 controls (37.5%). No dog showed clinical signs over 365 days

Claims citing this study

  • In a Brazilian shelter, one dose reduced urinary PCR positivity over a year but did not prevent it Leptospirosis (quadrivalent)
  • Vaccination reduces disease severity and shedding but does not prevent infection or shedding Leptospirosis (quadrivalent)
  • Shelter and sanctuary dogs can shed leptospires in urine without signs Leptospirosis (quadrivalent)
