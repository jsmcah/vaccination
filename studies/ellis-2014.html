ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Ellis 2014

Full reference
Ellis J, Rhodes C, Lacoste S, Krakowka S. 2014. Antibody responses to Bordetella bronchiseptica in vaccinated and infected dogs. Canadian Veterinary Journal 55:857-864.
Design
controlled serology study (no challenge, one unvaccinated contact control per group)
Species
dog
Animals
22 dogs vaccinated or observed plus banked sera. Group A 8 laboratory beagles (7 vaccinated, 1 contact control), Group B 14 client-owned dogs (9 vaccinated once, 5 household contact controls), Group C banked sera from 7 puppies and 12 kenneled beagles given earlier whole-cell or IN vaccines
Population
Group A were Bb-seropositive but unvaccinated adult beagles aged 2 to 3 years housed together at WCVM. Group B were healthy client-owned dogs from 5 multidog households in Calgary with variable Bb vaccination histories
Funding
independent. Supported by the principal author's discretionary funds (acknowledgements). Bulk vaccine antigen for gel analysis was obtained from the manufacturer (methods). Authors at University of Saskatchewan and Ohio State University (author affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Antibody outcomes only, with no challenge or clinical endpoint and one unvaccinated control in the laboratory group. Very small and heterogeneous client-owned group with variable prior exposure.
What it reports
In 7 Bb-seropositive beagles given acellular SC Bb vaccine (Bronchicine CAe) on days 0 and 14, all had increased Bb IgG, mostly after the first dose, and 5 of 7 had smaller IgA increases. Complement-mediated bactericidal activity of serum rose 20% to 56% after vaccination. In client-owned dogs given one dose, dogs with no Bb vaccine history had minimal responses, dogs given acellular vaccine 9 months earlier had 2- to 4-fold rises, and dogs with high titers after IN vaccination 6 to 9 months earlier did not respond further.
