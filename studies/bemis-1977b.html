ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Bemis 1977b

Full reference
Bemis DA, Carmichael LE, Appel MJG. 1977. Naturally occurring respiratory disease in a kennel caused by Bordetella bronchiseptica. Cornell Veterinarian 67:282-293.
Design
case series (longitudinal outbreak investigation in one closed breeding kennel, no vaccination studied)
Species
dog
Animals
closed colony of 300 to 400 beagles followed December 1973 to August 1975. Culture results include 81 of 95 dogs in the individually housed juvenile room, 69 of 115 in group-housed juvenile rooms, 9 of 73 young dogs and 2 of 44 older dogs in the main kennel
Population
beagles in the Cornell Animal Science Department breeding kennel, all ages, with Bb infection concentrated in pups shortly after weaning at about 5 weeks
Funding
independent. Whitehall Foundation and John M. Olin Foundation (acknowledgement). Authors at the James A. Baker Institute, Cornell University
Study's main limitation
Single kennel with descriptive counts only, and the statements about immunity duration rest on the authors' interpretation and a thesis rather than measured data.
What it reports
Bb infection usually began shortly after weaning, caused a moist cough lasting 1 to 2 weeks in 20% to 33% of animals per group, and organisms were shed for 2 to 3 months. Morbidity, duration and severity were greatest in dogs housed together in pens. Adults sampled repeatedly did not carry Bb despite constant exposure, except two newly purchased adult males that developed severe cough 10 days after entering the kennel. Viral causes were excluded by isolation and serology, apart from CHV in the first two episodes.
