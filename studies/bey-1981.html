ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Bey 1981

Full reference
Bey RF, Shade FJ, Goodnow RA, Johnson RC. 1981. Intranasal vaccination of dogs with live avirulent Bordetella bronchiseptica: correlation of serum agglutination titer and the formation of secretory IgA with protection against experimentally induced infectious tracheobronchitis. American Journal of Veterinary Research 42(7):1130-1132.
Design
experimental challenge study (two studies, onset of protection by challenge day, plus an unchallenged serology study)
Species
dog
Animals
94 dogs. Study I had 74 (51 IN vaccinates challenged on days 1, 2, 4, 5 and 14 in groups of 5, 5, 9, 12 and 20, and 23 unvaccinated controls in groups of 5, 8 and 10). Study II had 20 (10 IN vaccinates, 10 controls, not challenged)
Population
mixed-breed dogs 8 to 12 weeks old from rural sources, quarantined and confirmed free of Bb and CPIV by culture and serology, aerosol challenge with virulent strain D-2
Funding
manufacturer. Two of four authors (Shade, Goodnow) were at Burns Biotec Laboratories, the vaccine strain S-55 was prepared by their earlier method, no funding statement (author affiliations)
Study's main limitation
No statistical analysis, small groups per challenge day, and housing and observer blinding are not described. The IgA to protection link combines study II serology with study I challenge outcomes in different dogs.
What it reports
Protection from cough was 0% at 1 day (5 dogs), 20% at 2 days (5 dogs, same as controls at 20%), 56% at 4 days (9 dogs, controls 12%), 83% at 5 days (12 dogs) and 95% at 14 days (20 dogs, controls 0%). Nasal sIgA GMT rose from 1:2 to 1:8 at day 4, 1:64 at day 6, 1:157.6 at day 14 and 1:445.7 at day 21, and serum agglutinin rose only after day 6. Authors state that dogs with sIgA titers above 1:157 were more than 95% protected.
