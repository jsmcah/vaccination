ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Jacobs 1993

Full reference
Jacobs AA, Chalmers WS, Pasman J, van Vugt F, Cuenen LH. 1993. Feline bordetellosis: challenge and vaccine studies. Veterinary Record 133(11):260-263.
DOI
10.1136/vr.133.11.260
Design
experimental challenge study (aerosol Bordetella challenge, and an experimental fimbrial subunit vaccine vs control)
Species
cat
Animals
4 cats in the challenge study, 15 kittens in the vaccine study (10 vaccinated, 5 controls)
Population
8-week-old cats free of FCV, FHV and C. psittaci
Funding
probably manufacturer (authors affiliated with Intervet per later citations). Not confirmed without the PDF
Study's main limitation
Small groups, abstract only, and the vaccine was an experimental subunit product rather than the licensed live vaccine.
What it reports
Cats challenged with Bordetella as the sole pathogen developed nasal discharge, sneezing, coughing and rales within five days, lasting about 10 days. Two doses of a fimbrial subunit vaccine 2 weeks apart gave 95% to 100% protection against these signs.

Claims citing this study

  • Bordetella in shelter cats usually occurs with herpesvirus, calicivirus or Mycoplasma rather than alone Feline Bordetella (intranasal)
  • Bordetella is a primary feline pathogen that usually causes mild signs, with pneumonia mainly in young kittens Feline Bordetella (intranasal)
  • One intranasal dose reduces clinical signs after challenge 72 hours later Feline Bordetella (intranasal)
