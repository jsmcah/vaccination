ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Ellis 2001

Full reference
Ellis JA, Haines DM, West KH, Burr JH, Dayton A, Townsend HGG, Kanara EW, Konoby C, Crichlow A, Martin K, Headrick G. 2001. Effect of vaccination on experimental infection with Bordetella bronchiseptica in dogs. Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association 218(3):367-375.
Design
experimental challenge study (randomized, unvaccinated control, two replicates, blinded clinical observer)
Species
dog
Animals
50 SPF Beagle puppies (5 groups of 10, two replicates of 5 per group), 9 replicate-2 puppies seropositive before vaccination were excluded, leaving 41 in the pooled analysis
Population
SPF Beagle puppies from a commercial breeder, Bb culture and seronegative, vaccinated for CDV, CPV, CAV-2 and CPIV, first Bb vaccine at 14 weeks, aerosol challenge with a recent field isolate (Regina-1) at about 20 weeks
Funding
manufacturer. Supported by Pfizer Animal Health, four authors at Pfizer Animal Health, and both vaccines were Pfizer products (title page footnote and author affiliations)
Study's main limitation
Groups were housed in separate rooms with only two replicates, and replicate 2 had pre-existing Bb antibodies in 9 puppies, so the pooled analysis is unbalanced. Single challenge 2 weeks after the last dose at peak immunity.
What it reports
Mean percentage of days coughing (days 4 to 10 after challenge) was 77.7% in controls, 20.9% IM only (2 doses), 37.0% IN only (1 dose), 3.3% IM then IN, and 3.4% IN then IM. All vaccinated groups coughed significantly less than controls, and both sequential groups coughed significantly less than IN alone. No adverse effects after either vaccine. Serum antibody correlated negatively with Bb recovered from nose and pharynx.
