ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Scott-Garrard 2020

Full reference
Scott-Garrard M, Wang X, Chiang Y-W, David F. 2020. Thirteen-month duration of immunity of an oral canine vaccine against challenge with Bordetella bronchiseptica. Veterinary Record Open 5:e000423.
DOI
10.1136/vetreco-2020-000423
Design
experimental challenge study (randomized, placebo controlled, blinded)
Species
dog
Animals
34 dogs (17 oral Bb vaccine, 17 oral placebo)
Population
purpose-bred beagles about 8 weeks old at vaccination, seronegative and culture negative for B bronchiseptica before vaccination, housed in isolation by group until challenge
Funding
manufacturer. Sponsored by Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health, maker of Recombitek Oral Bordetella, and all authors are its employees (funding statement and author affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Single laboratory study of one product with 17 dogs per group, a heterologous aerosol challenge, and shedding measured at only one post-challenge time point. The vaccine was given at the minimum protective dose, below the commercial dose.
What it reports
A single oral dose of monovalent live avirulent Bb vaccine at about 8 weeks of age was followed by aerosol challenge 406 days later. 13 of 17 placebo dogs (76.5%) and 0 of 17 vaccinates developed cough on 2 or more consecutive days (prevented fraction 1.000, 95% CI 0.773 to 1.000). Mean days of cough was 7.8 in placebo dogs and 0.2 in vaccinates (mitigated fraction 0.841, 95% CI 0.668 to 1.000). Tracheal isolation 14 days after challenge was 88.2% versus 5.9% (PF 0.933, 95% CI 0.694 to 0.998) and nasal isolation 64.7% versus 5.9% (PF 0.909, 95% CI 0.501 to 0.997), with no vaccine adverse reactions.
