ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Cureton 2016

Full reference
Cureton DK, Scott-Garrard M, Parker DS, House A, Veal E, Aitcheson T, Chiang YW. 2016. An inactivated H3N2 canine influenza virus (CIV) vaccine aids in the prevention of clinical disease and virus shedding in dogs challenged with virulent H3N2 CIV. International Journal of Applied Research in Veterinary Medicine 14(2):128-134.
Design
experimental challenge study (vaccinate vs placebo)
Species
dog
Animals
30 beagles (10 per group, two antigen doses and placebo)
Population
seronegative commercial beagles 6 weeks old
Funding
manufacturer. Merial funded the study and all authors are at Merial Biological Research and Development (acknowledgements, affiliation)
Study's main limitation
Manufacturer study, small groups, and challenge only 7 days after dose 2.
What it reports
Two SQ doses 21 days apart, challenge by aerosol 7 days after dose 2. No vaccinate developed clinical disease versus 9 of 10 placebo dogs, and shedding was reduced (prevented fraction 80%). On day 14, 1 of 10 and 0 of 10 vaccinates had HI antibody, on day 21, 7 of 10 and 2 of 10, and all had by day 27.

Claims citing this study

  • Two doses of H3N2 vaccine reduced signs, shedding and lung lesions on challenge 1 to 3 weeks after the second dose H3N2 canine influenza
  • Few dogs have antibody 14 days after one dose of inactivated H3N2 vaccine H3N2 canine influenza
  • Vaccination reduces disease and shedding but does not prevent infection H3N2 canine influenza
  • Two doses 3 to 4 weeks apart are needed, starting after 8 weeks of age H3N2 canine influenza
  • Vaccinating at-risk dogs when H3N2 is locally identified reduces illness in those dogs H3N2 canine influenza
