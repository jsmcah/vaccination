ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Ellis 2011

Full reference
Ellis J, Anseeuw E, Gow S, Bryan H, Salb A, Goji N, Rhodes C, La Coste S, Smits J, Kutz S. 2011. Seroepidemiology of respiratory (group 2) canine coronavirus, canine parainfluenza virus, and Bordetella bronchiseptica infections in urban dogs in a humane shelter and in rural dogs in small communities. Canadian Veterinary Journal 52(8):861-868.
Design
prospective field serology study
Species
dog
Animals
142 shelter dogs and 216 rural or small-community dogs
Population
dogs entering the Winnipeg Humane Society and dogs in rural western Canadian communities
Funding
mixed. Supported in part by an unrestricted Pfizer Animal Health grant (acknowledgment)
Study's main limitation
One shelter, with antibody measured rather than protection.
What it reports
CPIV seroprevalence was low and did not differ between urban shelter and rural dogs. Antibody to CPIV, CRCoV or Bordetella on arrival was not associated with sparing of respiratory disease in the shelter.
