ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Classe 2024

Full reference
Classe HM, Dant JC, Mogler M, Stachura KA, LaFleur RL, Xu Z, Tarpey I. 2024. Efficacy and safety in dogs following administration of an alphavirus RNA particle canine influenza H3N2 vaccine. Vaccines 12(10):1138.
DOI
10.3390/vaccines12101138
Design
experimental challenge study (with separate field safety trial)
Species
dog
Animals
40 beagles in the challenge study (20 vaccinated, 20 placebo) and 654 dogs (1301 doses) in the field safety trial
Population
seronegative purpose-bred beagles 7 to 8 weeks old, and client-owned dogs in five regions
Funding
manufacturer. All authors are Merck or MSD Animal Health employees. Paper states no external funding and no conflicts
Study's main limitation
Manufacturer authorship, challenge 3 weeks after dose 2 only, and an uncontrolled safety trial.
What it reports
Two SQ doses 21 days apart. All 20 vaccinates seroconverted after dose 2. On challenge 3 weeks later, vaccination reduced the duration of clinical signs, the duration and amount of shedding, lung consolidation and suppurative pneumonia. In the field trial lethargy was the most common adverse event (1.6% of doses).

Claims citing this study

  • Two doses of H3N2 vaccine reduced signs, shedding and lung lesions on challenge 1 to 3 weeks after the second dose H3N2 canine influenza
  • Vaccination reduces disease and shedding but does not prevent infection H3N2 canine influenza
  • CIV vaccines have adverse event rates no higher than other canine vaccines H3N2 canine influenza
  • Two doses 3 to 4 weeks apart are needed, starting after 8 weeks of age H3N2 canine influenza
