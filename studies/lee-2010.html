ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Lee 2010

Full reference
Lee C, Jung K, Oh J, Oh T, Han S, Hwang J, Yeom M, Son D, Kim J, Park B, Moon H, Song D, Kang B. 2010. Protective efficacy and immunogenicity of an inactivated avian-origin H3N2 canine influenza vaccine in dogs challenged with the virulent virus. Veterinary Microbiology 143(2-4):184-188.
DOI
10.1016/j.vetmic.2009.11.037
Design
experimental challenge study (vaccinate vs control)
Species
dog
Animals
10 beagle puppies (4 vaccinated, 4 unvaccinated challenged, 2 unchallenged)
Population
seronegative conventional beagle puppies 10 weeks old in Korea
Funding
manufacturer. Corresponding authors at Green Cross Veterinary Products research unit, others at Animal Genetics Inc (affiliations). No funding statement
Study's main limitation
Four vaccinates, an experimental Korean vaccine given IM, and challenge 2 weeks after dose 2.
What it reports
Two IM doses 2 weeks apart, challenge 2 weeks after dose 2. Vaccinates showed no clinical signs, milder lung lesions, and shedding from 1 to 4 days after challenge versus 1 to 8 days in controls. HI titers exceeded 64 in vaccinates 21 days after the first dose.

Claims citing this study

  • Two doses of H3N2 vaccine reduced signs, shedding and lung lesions on challenge 1 to 3 weeks after the second dose H3N2 canine influenza
  • Vaccination reduces disease and shedding but does not prevent infection H3N2 canine influenza
  • Vaccinating at-risk dogs when H3N2 is locally identified reduces illness in those dogs H3N2 canine influenza
