ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Altman 2017

Full reference
Altman KD, Kelman M, Ward MP. 2017. Are vaccine strain, type or administration protocol risk factors for canine parvovirus vaccine failure? Veterinary Microbiology 210:8-16.
DOI
10.1016/j.vetmic.2017.08.019
Design
retrospective observational study (national surveillance case reports, cases only)
Species
dog
Animals
594 apparent vaccination failure cases (of 4960 CPV cases)
Population
dogs reported to the Australian Disease WatchDog surveillance system with CPV after vaccination with an identified vaccine, 2010 to 2014
Funding
manufacturer. Virbac Australia, a vaccine maker, provided access to its Disease WatchDog database (acknowledgements). No funding statement. Honours dissertation of the first author
Study's main limitation
Cases only, with no data on vaccine market share, so over-representation of a vaccine type cannot be read as higher failure risk.
What it reports
Vaccine strain (CPV-2 versus CPV-2b) was not associated with vaccination failure. 561 (96.9%) failures followed attenuated and 18 (3.1%) killed vaccines, with no significant difference by type. Mean vaccination-to-disease interval was shorter for killed vaccines (1.73 weeks) than attenuated (9.31 weeks), P = 0.0628. A later age at last vaccination was associated with lower failure risk, supporting a final dose at 16 weeks or later.
