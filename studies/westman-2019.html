ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Westman 2019

Full reference
Westman M, Norris J, Malik R, Hofmann-Lehmann R, Harvey A, McLuckie A, Perkins M, Schofield D, Marcus A, McDonald M, Ward M, Hall E, Sheehy P, Hosie M. 2019. The diagnosis of feline leukaemia virus (FeLV) infection in owned and group-housed rescue cats in Australia. Viruses 11(6):503.
DOI
10.3390/v11060503
Design
cross-sectional field study with outbreak investigation
Species
cat
Animals
529
Population
440 healthy owned cats with outdoor access and 89 cats in two group-housed rescue facilities near Sydney tested after index cases
Funding
Australian Companion Animal Health Foundation and Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health Australia. Test kits donated by IDEXX, Zoetis and BioNote
Study's main limitation
Single time point testing, so some infection classes are presumptive. Timing of infection in the rescue cats is unknown
What it reports
Progressive infection was found in 2 of 440 (0.5%) owned cats and 19 of 89 (21%) rescue cats, with regressive infection in 22 of 89 (25%). Rescue group 3 had median age 1 year and 24% progressive infection.

Claims citing this study

  • FeLV transmission among individually housed shelter cats has not been documented, but outbreaks with many infected cats have occurred in group-housed rescue facilities FeLV
  • In long-term group housing, FeLV can infect a large share of young cats once infected cats are present FeLV
