ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Monteiro 2016

Full reference
Monteiro FL, Cargnelutti JF, Martins M, Anziliero D, Erhardt MM, Weiblen R, Flores EF. 2016. Detection of respiratory viruses in shelter dogs maintained under varying environmental conditions. Brazilian Journal of Microbiology 47:876-881.
DOI
10.1016/j.bjm.2016.07.002
Design
cross-sectional serosurvey (virus detection by PCR on nasal swabs, not serology, three shelters)
Species
dog
Animals
186 dogs (74, 35 and 77 in shelters 1, 2 and 3, about 50% of each population)
Population
dogs in three shelters in Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, sampled in July and August 2014 (cold season), one shelter with poor sanitation, nutrition and high density, two with fair to good conditions
Funding
independent. Five authors are CNPq research fellows, no other funding stated, no conflicts declared (acknowledgements and conflict of interest statement)
Study's main limitation
Three shelters only, so environmental conditions are entirely confounded with shelter and no statistical comparison is possible. Bb and clinical signs per dog were not assessed, and vaccination history was not reported.
What it reports
At least one respiratory virus was detected in 78% (58/74) of dogs in the poor-condition shelter, mainly CPIV (71%) often with CAdV-2 or CDV, compared with 14% (5/35) and 9% (7/77) in the two better shelters, with only single infections there. Authors attribute the difference to density, sanitation and nutrition. Bb was not tested.
