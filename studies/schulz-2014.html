ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Schulz 2014

Full reference
Schulz BS, Kurz S, Weber K, Balzer HJ, Hartmann K. 2014. Detection of respiratory viruses and Bordetella bronchiseptica in dogs with acute respiratory tract infections. The Veterinary Journal 201(3):365-369.
DOI
10.1016/j.tvjl.2014.04.019
Design
cross-sectional serosurvey (PCR detection in sick versus healthy dogs, not serology)
Species
dog
Animals
151 dogs (61 with CIRD, 90 clinically healthy)
Population
dogs in southern Germany sampled July 2011 to July 2012 at a university clinic, a private practice, an animal shelter and a research kennel. Of the CIRD dogs 16 lived in a shelter and 45 were privately owned. Healthy dogs came from a shelter (13), a research kennel (29) and private homes (48)
Funding
not stated. No funding statement. Viral PCR was done by Vet Med Labor (IDEXX), which employs coauthor Balzer and is stated to have had no role in design or interpretation (conflict of interest statement). No vaccine maker involved
Study's main limitation
Convenience sample from mostly one region with a single sampling time per dog, and Bb vaccination history was almost absent (no CIRD dog and one healthy dog vaccinated), so the study says nothing about vaccine effect.
What it reports
Bb was detected by PCR in 78.7% (48/61, 95% CI 66.7 to 87.2) of dogs with CIRD and 45.6% (41/90, 95% CI 35.7 to 55.8) of healthy dogs (P < 0.001). Among Bb-positive CIRD dogs 52.1% had Bb as the only agent detected. Privately owned dogs were Bb positive more often than shelter or kennel dogs (69.9%, 65/93, versus 41.4%, 24/58, P < 0.001).
