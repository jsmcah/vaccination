ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Thrusfield 1989

Full reference
Thrusfield MV, Aitken CGG, Muirhead RH. 1989. A field investigation of kennel cough. Efficacy of vaccination. Journal of Small Animal Practice 30(10):550-560.
DOI
10.1111/j.1748-5827.1989.tb01471.x
Design
retrospective observational case-control study (postal questionnaire to randomly sampled UK practices)
Species
dog
Animals
1,847 dogs analyzed (1,354 cases and 493 high-risk controls), from 2,456 returned questionnaires
Population
kennel cough cases and non-coughing 'high risk' control dogs (exposed to kennels, grooming or dog shows) seen by 563 randomly selected BSAVA member practices in the UK, summers of 1986 and 1987
Funding
manufacturer. Financial support from C-Vet Ltd, Coopers Animal Health Ltd, Mycofarm UK Ltd and Norden Laboratories (acknowledgements). Authors at the University of Edinburgh
Study's main limitation
Vaccination status and case status were practitioner reported with no laboratory confirmation, and controls were selected by practitioners later in the year. Low response gave large standard errors, and estimates are relative to dogs vaccinated against CDV only, with no unvaccinated reference group.
What it reports
In a logistic model relative to CDV-only dogs, the main-effect log odds change for Bb vaccine alone was -0.3 (SE 0.48), not significant, and depended on the adenovirus vaccine given. For common combinations, CDV + CAV-2 + Bb gave -2.0 (SE 0.32), CDV + live CAV-1 + Bb gave -5.3 (SE 1.04), and CDV + inactivated CAV-1 + Bb gave -1.8 (SE 0.33). Adding both Bb and CPiV gave -2.3 (SE 0.45) with live CAV-1, -1.7 (SE 0.85) with CAV-2, and 0.1 (SE 0.61) with inactivated CAV-1, which the authors describe as a positive statistical interaction between Bb and CPiV vaccines.
