ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Johnson 1983

Full reference
Johnson RP, Povey RC. 1983. Transfer and decline of maternal antibody to feline calicivirus. Canadian Veterinary Journal 24(1):6-9.
Design
experimental study (natural transfer and decline of maternal antibody in a barrier colony, no vaccination of kittens, no challenge)
Species
cat
Animals
12 kittens from 4 FCV-immune queens, plus 7 control kittens and their unimmunized dams
Population
kittens of queens from a barrier maintained colony free of known feline respiratory viruses, made immune by experimental FCV 255 infection 3 to 6 months before parturition, sampled weekly to 15 weeks
Funding
mixed. Canadian Veterinary Medical Research Trust Fund (acknowledgments). A title page footnote gives Norden Laboratories, a vaccine maker, as an address for an author. The work formed part of a PhD thesis at Guelph
Study's main limitation
Twelve kittens from four queens, with queen immunity from experimental infection rather than vaccination, and antibody only.
What it reports
Kittens acquired FCV SN antibody mainly through colostrum. At one week of age their titres approached or equalled those of their dams, with a mean transmission of 65% at one week. Maternal antibody declined to undetected levels between 10 and 14 weeks of age (3 kittens by 11 weeks, 8 by 12 weeks, the remaining 4 by 14 weeks). Estimated half life was about 15 days. Persistence was shorter in kittens with lower one week titres.

Claims citing this study

  • FCV maternal antibody wanes to undetectable levels between 10 and 14 weeks of age. MLV FVRCP
  • Repeating vaccination until at least 14 weeks covers most kittens as FCV maternal antibody wanes. MLV FVRCP
  • FCV maternal antibody wanes to undetectable levels between 10 and 14 weeks of age. Intranasal herpesvirus and calicivirus (MLV)
  • FCV maternal antibody wanes to undetectable levels between 10 and 14 weeks of age. Dual-strain killed calicivirus
