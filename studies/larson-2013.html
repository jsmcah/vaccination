ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Larson 2013

Full reference
Larson LJ, Thiel BE, Sharp P, Schultz RD. 2013. A comparative study of protective immunity provided by oral, intranasal and parenteral canine Bordetella bronchiseptica vaccines. International Journal of Applied Research in Veterinary Medicine 11(3):153-160.
Design
experimental challenge study (randomized, saline control, pathologist blinded for lung scores)
Species
dog
Animals
40 dogs (10 oral Bronchi-Shield ORAL, 10 IN Bronchi-Shield III, 10 SC Bronchicine CAe two doses, 10 saline controls)
Population
beagle pups 6 to 8 weeks old from Ridglan Farms, culture negative and with low or no Bb antibody at vaccination, aerosol challenged with three pooled virulent Bb strains on study day 42
Funding
manufacturer. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica, maker of the oral and IN vaccines tested, provided funding (acknowledgements). Authors at University of Wisconsin-Madison (author affiliation)
Study's main limitation
No statistical tests are reported, so group differences rest on descriptive means with n=10 per group. Funded by the maker of the oral and IN products, with the competitor SC product as comparator.
What it reports
Oral and IN live vaccines were given once on study day 14 and the SC killed vaccine on days 0 and 14, so challenge on day 42 was 28 days after the last dose in every group. Oral and IN groups both had mean cumulative clinical scores of 0.03, with 70% of dogs in each not coughing at all. In the SC group, 7 of 10 dogs had spontaneous harsh cough for several days (mean score 0.8), and in controls 9 of 10 had harsh ongoing cough and 2 required euthanasia (mean score 2.0). All dogs were tracheal culture positive at necropsy on day 56.
