ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

Ohe 2007

Full reference
Ohe K, Sakai S, Takahasi T, Sunaga F, Murakami M, Kiuchi A, Fukuyama M, Furuhata K, Hara M, Ishikawa Y, Taneno A. 2007. Genogrouping of vaccine breakdown strains (VBS) of feline calicivirus in Japan. Veterinary Research Communications 31(4):497-507.
DOI
10.1007/s11259-007-3454-1
Design
case series (laboratory characterization of 15 FCV isolates from vaccinated cats, capsid sequencing, genogrouping and in vitro neutralization, no challenge, no unvaccinated comparison)
Species
cat
Animals
15 FCV isolates from 15 vaccinated cats (7 F9, 6 FCV-255, 2 FC-7 vaccinated)
Population
client-owned and animal protection centre cats in Japan with respiratory or other FCV disease within 1 year of vaccination (one persistently infected), isolated 1993 to 2003
Funding
manufacturer. Two of 11 authors at Central Research Laboratories, Intervet Co., Tokyo (author affiliations). No funding statement
Study's main limitation
Only 15 isolates and no denominator of vaccinated cats that stayed well, so it cannot show that any vaccine strain made cats more or less susceptible. Neutralization used one hyperimmune cat anti-F9 serum and one rabbit anti-FCV-255 serum, not sera from vaccinated cats.
What it reports
Of 7 isolates from F9-vaccinated cats, 2 were genogroup I and 5 genogroup II. Of 6 from FCV-255-vaccinated cats, 5 were genogroup I and 1 genogroup II. The 2 FC-7 isolates split 1 and 1 (p < 0.05 or p = 0.05, reported both ways). Isolates shared 70.6% to 82.9% sequence identity with the vaccine strains and none were vaccine derived. Anti-F9 serum neutralized isolates at mean 1:18.0 (about 60-fold below homologous) and anti-FCV-255 serum at mean <1:40.5 (about 200-fold below), with no difference by genogroup. The authors suggest alternating F9 and FCV-255 vaccines or a polyvalent vaccine containing genogroup II strains.
