What the labels mean

Which vaccines

Core
Considered essential for all animals in that context category, and should be given at intake or as soon as possible. (Guideline definition)
Situational
May have good evidence for use under particular circumstances, but not appropriate for all shelter or community animals. (Guideline definition)

How certain the evidence is

Evidence rating
The task force's own rating of the evidence behind each recommendation, from 0 to 4, recorded when the recommendations were voted. Higher means more certain. A range means the statement rests on more than one voted recommendation and they were rated differently.
Not rated
No rating was recorded for this statement, usually because it was proposed after the vote.

Kinds of evidence

  • ■ Experimental
  • ● Observational
  • ▲ Case report or series
  • ◆ Expert or consensus
  • ■ filled: independent or not stated   □ hollow: manufacturer funded, authored, or supplied
  • (species) study in a different species
  • (model) decision model

Shelter dogs

Not advised for shelter dogs because it contains no parainfluenza, an important cause of outbreaks in closely housed dogs Evidence 3 of 4

Shelters admit puppies younger than 7 weeks
If it does not hold

Draws on the biology of: Route

Balance. Oral monovalent vaccine reduces Bb disease after challenge about as well as IN in two sponsor studies and less well in a third, but it gives no parainfluenza protection and is labeled only from 8 weeks. Favors multivalent IN products for shelter dogs.

Basis. Route evidence is weak and conflicting (manufacturer studies). The parainfluenza reasoning is consensus.

Community dogs

Experience only
Premise a shelter can check
The dog uses these services
If it does not hold

Draws on the biology of: Route, Duration of immunity

Balance. One oral dose reduces cough after challenge for about a year in manufacturer studies, and it can give access to grooming and boarding. It leaves dogs without parainfluenza protection. Reasonable where a service requirement is the main reason to vaccinate.

Basis. Challenge efficacy is evidence-based (manufacturer studies). The service-access reasoning is consensus.
