ASV shelter vaccination guidelines: evidence

One MLV dose for adult shelter animals

The guideline advises one MLV core vaccine dose for adult cats and dogs at or before shelter intake, with a second dose optional or limited to particular conditions. This page gathers the claims from the pathogen ledgers that bear on that change. Each claim also appears on its vaccine page.

One shelter. Recombinant use was clustered in the second outbreak, and in a sensitivity analysis the gap narrowed with longer follow-up (98% versus 94% at 28 days or more)

Limits of the evidence for this claim
Small counts (5 of 97, 1 of 10, 2 of 239). In Kreisler 2026, 11 of 12 MLV first-dose non-responders eventually seroconverted, some only after 4 or more doses, and titer testing was needed to find them
Canine parainfluenza No study compares one with two parenteral doses of CPIV Not assembled
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Licensing studies (Emery 1976, Kontor 1981) used two parenteral doses with no single-dose arm
Canine parvovirus Nearly all shelter dogs have CPV antibody 1 to 2 weeks after one MLV dose at intake ● (dog)
Limits of the evidence for this claim
About 95% positive by day 6 to 8 and 98% to 100% by day 13 to 15, but 69% to 84% were positive before vaccination. Point-of-care antibody test, no unvaccinated group
Canine parvovirus One MLV dose protected all seronegative dogs against challenge 3 to 5 weeks later □ (dog), □ (dog)
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Five and 18 vaccinates. Both manufacturer studies in young antibody-free dogs, not shelter adults
Feline calicivirus No study compares one with two MLV FCV doses in adult cats, and licensing studies test two doses. □
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Brunner 2005 compares MLV with and without FeLV vaccine and reports group median titers only, with no one versus two dose comparison.
Feline herpesvirus Few cats seroconvert to FHV after one parenteral MLV dose □, ■
Limits of the evidence for this claim
2 of 11 SPF kittens seroconverted after the first dose and 7 of 11 after the second (Jas 2009). FHV antibody is a weak correlate of protection, and cell-mediated immunity matters.
Feline herpesvirus Parenteral FHV vaccines were tested after two doses, so whether one dose suffices is unknown □
Limits of the evidence for this claim
This is an absence of single-dose data, not evidence that a second dose is needed. The second dose is usually given before antibody from the first would have risen.

Limits of the evidence for this claim
9 of 109 (8.3%, 95% CI 4.2 to 15.1) had a four-fold rise, and 40.9% had antibody before vaccination. Antibody only, 28-day follow-up.
Feline herpesvirus Many adult shelter cats already carry FHV, so vaccinating them adds little Consensus
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Based on meeting experience. It sits against DiGangi 2012, where 89% of incoming cats lacked FHV antibody.
Premise a shelter can check
Most adult intake has prior FHV infection
If it does not hold
Where many adults are FHV naive, an intake dose may reduce disease
Panleukopenia Previously vaccinated cats stay protected for years, and a booster adds nothing for cats that already have high titers ○, ○
Limits of the evidence for this claim
Antibody outcome. High-titer cats showed no titer rise after a booster (Bergmann 2018). The standard explanation, not tested in these studies, is that existing antibody neutralizes the modified live virus before it can replicate
